Files, but somewhere else

We asked the question nobody had thought to ask: what if your stuff lived on a drive that isn't in your hand, and your friends could see it too? Revolutionary. It appears in the Finder sidebar under Locations. That's the whole invention.

Drag. Drop. Leave the building.

Put a card of rushes on the pad, pick where it lands, quit the app, go get a coffee, go get a mortgage. The hand-off keeps going without you, because we taught it object permanence.

Your SSD, exhumed

Here's the thing nobody tells you: every time you open a file off any shared drive, your Mac keeps a copy "just in case." Hundreds of gigabytes of just in case. We name the culprits and hand it all back in one click. Giving is believing.
Your drives

Zero drives? Fine. A hundred? Also fine.

Run one drive, run a hundred, or run none at all and enjoy a beautifully empty shelf — your call, we don't judge. Every drive is a pebble that fills up as your media does, because a picture of a nearly-full drive lands harder than a number does. Each one gets an eyeball: click it and the drive leaves Finder, click it again and it comes back. That is the entire user manual. We're as surprised as you are.

Hand-offs

The spy thumb drive, minus the car chase.

You know the scene: one thumb drive holds everything, and three men in suits chase you through a parking garage to get it. You don't need the thumb drive anymore. Drop 512 GB of dailies at 7pm and go home — it finishes without you, encrypted in transit and at rest, on a drive only the people you ticked can even see. Let them search your bag.
The entire status readout
"5 items → the-bear-transfer · started 2m ago · keeps going if you quit." That's it. No queue depth, no retry counter, no progress bar inventing a number to make you feel better.
Space

Look at these graphs. Look at them.

There are bars. There is a ring. They are colour-coded and, frankly, gorgeous. They also happen to show which drives your Mac has been quietly hoarding copies of — open a file once and it keeps one forever, apparently — and hand every gigabyte back in one click while the real media sits untouched on the drive. But mostly: pretty, aren't they?
People

If you're the boss, you decide everyone's fate.

Who gets what, when they get in, and how they get out. Tick the drives a person sees and that becomes their entire universe — they sign in, those drives appear in their Finder, nothing else exists. Lock a finished drive read-only and nobody writes to it again, you included, which is either a safeguard or a punishment depending on the day.

Finder

Down the left, where drives live.

You'll find your SpaceTime drives on the left in Finder, under Locations, ready to be filled with your dreams — or your abysmal first assembly. A hexagon, because it reads as a volume and not a folder. A menu-bar mark, so one glance tells you everything's ready.
